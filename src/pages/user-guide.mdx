# User Guide

Solar Forecast shows a solar radiation forecast (Global Horizontal
Irradiation, or GHI), sunshine duration, UV index, and temperature, powered
by [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com).

## Data refresh

Open-Meteo blends several national weather models, and each one updates on
its own schedule, for example ECMWF every 6 hours and NOAA GFS roughly
hourly. Which model applies to your location, and therefore how often the
underlying forecast actually changes, depends on where you are.

Every time this page loads, it fetches the latest data directly from
Open-Meteo (never a cached snapshot), and then re-fetches automatically on
the interval you set with the **Refresh** control. Refreshing more often
than the underlying model updates won't reveal new data, it just checks
again for whenever Open-Meteo's next update lands.

## Grid accuracy

Weather models don't forecast individual addresses; they forecast a grid of
points, with spacing that depends on the model (roughly 9-25 km for global
models like ECMWF, down to a few km for higher-resolution regional ones).
Open-Meteo snaps whatever coordinates you request to the nearest grid point
of the model it selects, so the forecast you see represents that whole grid
cell, not your exact spot.

The footer's **Grid** row shows the coordinates Open-Meteo actually used,
which can differ slightly from what you entered. Click it to see exactly
how far off it is on the map.

## Customization

### Location

By default the forecast is for Dublin. To change it, use the controls in the
top navigation bar:

- **Lat,Lon field**: type coordinates as `latitude,longitude` and press
  Enter. Clearing the field and pressing Enter resets to Dublin.
- **Location button** (the crosshair icon): asks your browser for your
  current location and switches the forecast to it.
- Clicking the **Lat,Lon** label opens the current coordinates in Google
  Maps.

You can also set the location directly in the URL, for example
`?lat=51.5074&lon=-0.1278`.

### Scale (units)

At the bottom of the page, the **Scale** row lets you switch how the GHI
values are displayed:

- **kWh/m²** (the default) shows plain energy output.
- **MJ/m²** shows the raw value as reported by Open-Meteo.
- The input field next to it accepts a custom multiplier, for example to
  roughly match your observed daily generation capacity (`?scale=5`).

### Forecast window and refresh

Also in the footer:

- **Forecast Days**: how many days of daily data to show (7 or 14, or a
  custom number).
- **Forecast Hours**: how many hours of hourly data to show, counted from
  yesterday (72 or 96, or a custom number).
- **Refresh**: how often the page automatically re-fetches data, in minutes
  (10, 30, 60, or a custom number).

Each of these can also be set directly in the URL, for example
`?forecast_days=7&forecast_hours=96&refresh=10`.

## Chart y-axis scaling

The y-axis on every chart is scaled to the range of the values shown, not
from zero. This makes differences between bars easier to see and lets you
read the value range straight off the y-axis, but it also means the bar
with the lowest value can look almost empty, since it barely rises above
the axis minimum:

![Daily GHI chart with a low day near the y-axis minimum](/assets/chart-daily-ghi.png)

## Adding to your home screen

On iPhone, open the site in Safari, tap the share icon, then **Add to Home
Screen** to get an app-like icon and launch experience.

Android has a similar option: open the site in Chrome, tap the three-dot
menu, then choose **Add to Home screen**.
